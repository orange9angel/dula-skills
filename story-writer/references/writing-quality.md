# Writing Quality

Use this reference to turn a premise or research brief into a short episode rather than a sequence of explanatory lines.

## Story Engine

State these six items before drafting:

1. **Want**: what the protagonist wants right now, in concrete terms.
2. **Blind spot**: the mistaken belief or habit that makes the want harder to achieve.
3. **Rule**: the gadget, situation, or social rule that creates repeatable consequences.
4. **Escalation**: at least two attempts whose outcomes make the next attempt necessary.
5. **Turn**: a discovery or choice that cannot be undone by simply walking away.
6. **Payoff**: a consequence that resolves the want and reinterprets an earlier detail.

If a beat can be removed without changing the following beat, the causal chain is weak. Replace or connect it.

## Research To Drama

- Extract only facts that can change a character's decision, create a constraint, or sharpen a joke.
- Prefer one accurate, surprising detail over a paragraph of exposition.
- Separate verified facts from interpretation. Never make a character cite a source unless citation is part of the scene.
- For established franchises, use research to preserve character relationships and world rules, then write an original plot and original dialogue. Do not reproduce episode transcripts or imitate a named writer's exact style.
- For current events or real people, avoid fabricated quotes and make the tone proportionate to the subject.

## Comedy

Choose one primary comic engine and one supporting device:

- **Status reversal**: the confident character loses control while the underestimated one gains leverage.
- **Rule exploitation**: a clear rule is used correctly in an absurdly literal way.
- **Escalating repetition**: repeat a pattern three times, changing stakes or meaning each time.
- **Misdirection**: make the audience predict a plausible result, then reveal a better causal result.
- **Dramatic irony**: the audience knows what one character does not.
- **Callback**: plant a harmless detail early and make it decisive later.
- **Reaction gap**: let one character treat chaos as ordinary while another panics.

Do not substitute meme references, random shouting, humiliation, or repeated catchphrases for setup and payoff. A joke should reveal character or alter the situation.

## Meaning

- Express the theme through a choice and its cost, not a closing lecture.
- Let the resolution complicate the protagonist's original belief rather than flipping it mechanically.
- Keep a comic ending comic. A small image, callback, or behavior change usually carries more weight than a narrator stating the lesson.
- Avoid `从此他明白了...` unless deliberate parody makes the explicit moral itself the joke.

## Dialogue And Performance

- Give each speaker a different objective in the exchange.
- Remove greetings, agreement, and repeated information unless they create rhythm or tension.
- Replace descriptions of emotion with behavior, evasion, contradiction, interruption, or a registered action.
- Read every line aloud mentally. Shorten lines that contain two ideas.
- Preserve character voice through vocabulary, sentence length, confidence, and what the character refuses to say.
- Use narration for compression and transitions, not to explain action already visible on screen.

## Candidate Selection

Score each outline from 1 to 5 on these dimensions before choosing it:

| Dimension | Strong evidence |
|---|---|
| Premise | The conflict is clear in one sentence and specific to these characters. |
| Causality | Each attempt changes the situation and forces the next beat. |
| Character | The plot emerges from a want, flaw, or relationship rather than coincidence. |
| Comedy or insight | Setups earn payoffs; the theme appears in action. |
| Visual potential | Important beats can be staged with registered assets and readable reactions. |
| Ending | The climax resolves the initial want and pays off an earlier detail. |

Reject a candidate with any score below `3`. Prefer the candidate with the clearest causal engine, not the largest number of events.

## Critic Pass

After the first complete draft, review it once from six perspectives:

1. Can the premise be understood by entry 3?
2. Does the middle escalate, or merely repeat at the same intensity?
3. Could another character say each line unchanged? If yes, strengthen voice.
4. Are comic or thematic payoffs visibly planted?
5. Does every technical tag correspond to something the audience can perceive?
6. Does the last beat land through action, image, or callback instead of explanation?

Rewrite the weakest beat and its setup together. Then rerun structural and registry validation.
